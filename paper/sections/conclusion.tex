\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The main thing this paper found is that the quality signal a scheduler is rewarded on matters more than any single scheduler design choice we tested. Grounding utility in measured downstream segmentation performance instead of pixel fidelity took MPC's advantage over the best fixed-depth baseline from a marginal $1.2\%$ to $32.2\%$, because pixel fidelity barely moves across the depth range studied here while task-relevant quality moves a lot ($12\%$ vs.\ $247\%$). A scheduler is only as good as what it's being asked to optimize.

With that signal fixed, a single unified utility function replaced five scoring definitions that used to disagree, and the ten-scheduler comparison under a routing-sensitive capacity regime showed the strongest variant (\texttt{mpc-2level}, routing and depth solved as peers) landing within $0.5\%$ of the offline optimality bound. The more interesting result was architectural: the $\approx 5$-point utility gap between the strongest and weakest MPC variants tracks admission control -- whether a scheduler is willing to drop an overwhelmed task rather than let it rot in queue -- not routing sophistication. The dedicated routing optimizer's own contribution tops out at $+0.84\%$ utility and disappears once link rates aren't scarce. Freezing a concrete route for more than a few minutes also doesn't survive this constellation's short ground-contact windows, no matter how the freeze period is tuned. And a learned (PPO) policy traded a consistent $5$--$8\%$ utility gap for $250$--$400\times$ less compute time per decision, with a catch nobody had predicted going in: without a congestion feature in its observation, its apparent robustness to link scarcity turns out to be accidental rather than adaptive.

Several things here are flagged rather than treated as settled. The uncompressed-reference mIoU used to anchor the quality signal hasn't been checked against FLAIR-1's official test split (\S\ref{sec:downstream}). Whether \texttt{mpc-hier} is the variant Xuanhao asked to drop is still pending his confirmation (\S\ref{sec:utility-results}). Both link-rate sweeps in \S\ref{sec:sweeps} were run against a placeholder quality table and need to be rerun against the real one before their exact numbers are final. The proposed satellite-count and ground-station-count sweeps, and retraining PPO under the unified reward, are next steps that need no new code, just more runs. None of that changes the central conclusion -- what a scheduler is optimized for matters before how well it optimizes it -- but resolving it would sharpen every number downstream of that conclusion.
